Why Recycling Should Be Mandatory

Use these ten subtitles to plan your essay. For each section, answer the guiding question in your own words, then explain your idea with a real or imagined example. The example sentences below are starting points: change them to fit your argument.

  1. Introduction: Why Recycling Matters

    What to write: Introduce the problem and state your opinion clearly.

    Example: “Every day, people throw away materials that could be used again. Recycling should be mandatory because it can reduce waste and protect the environment.”

  2. The Growing Problem of Waste

    What to write: Describe what happens when people throw away too much. Think about waste from homes, schools, and shops.

    Example: “A school cafeteria may throw away hundreds of plastic bottles each week, even though many of them can be recycled.”

  3. How Recycling Protects the Environment

    What to write: Explain how collecting and reusing materials can help keep places clean and reduce harm to wildlife.

    Example: “When bottles are recycled instead of littered, fewer pieces of plastic may end up in rivers and parks.”

  4. Saving Natural Resources

    What to write: Name a material that comes from nature and explain why reusing it helps.

    Example: “Recycling paper means some paper products can be made from used paper instead of only from new wood.”

  5. Reducing Pollution and Greenhouse Gases

    What to write: Explain how making products from recycled materials can sometimes use less energy than making them from raw materials. Find a reliable source if you add a statistic.

    Example: “Recycling aluminum cans can reduce the need to mine and process new metal, which takes energy.”

  6. Keeping Landfills from Filling Up

    What to write: Describe how separating recyclable items can leave less rubbish for landfills.

    Example: “If a neighborhood puts clean cardboard in recycling bins, less of that cardboard goes into its regular trash.”

  7. How Mandatory Recycling Could Work

    What to write: Suggest a practical rule. Explain what people would recycle and what support they would need.

    Example: “The city could provide clearly labeled bins for paper, metal, and plastic, along with simple instructions for every household.”

  8. Benefits for Communities and the Economy

    What to write: Discuss a possible benefit for the people in your community, such as cleaner public spaces or work collecting and sorting materials.

    Example: “A community recycling program could keep playgrounds cleaner and create work for people who collect and sort materials.”

  9. Challenges and Possible Solutions

    What to write: Acknowledge one difficulty and propose a fair way to address it.

    Example: “Some families may not know which plastics can be recycled, so the city should give residents a clear guide before enforcing the rule.”

  10. Conclusion: A Shared Responsibility

    What to write: Restate your opinion and remind readers of your strongest reasons. End with a clear final thought.

    Example: “Mandatory recycling could help communities waste less and use resources more carefully. With clear rules and convenient bins, everyone can take part.”

Writing tip: Turn each subtitle into a paragraph. Start with your main point, add a specific example or fact, then explain how it supports your argument. Check your teacher’s required length and cite any facts you research.
