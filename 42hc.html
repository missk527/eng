Is Technology Improving or Damaging Communication?

Use these ten subtitles to plan a balanced essay. Consider both benefits and drawbacks, then decide which argument you find stronger. For each section, answer the guiding question in your own words and add a specific example.

  1. Introduction: How We Communicate Today

    What to write: Introduce communication technologies and state your answer to the question, or say what factors your essay will weigh.

    Example: “Messages and video calls make communication faster, but they can also change the quality of our conversations.”

  2. Staying Connected Across Distances

    What to write: Explain how technology helps people maintain relationships when they cannot meet in person.

    Example: “A student can video call a grandparent who lives in another country.”

  3. Sharing Information Quickly

    What to write: Show when fast messages are useful for coordinating plans or sending important updates.

    Example: “A class group chat can let students know that a meeting time has changed.”

  4. Giving More People a Voice

    What to write: Discuss how digital tools can help people express ideas or take part in conversations, including those who find speaking in a group difficult.

    Example: “A quiet student may feel more comfortable contributing a thoughtful comment in an online class discussion.”

  5. Misunderstandings in Text Messages

    What to write: Explain how a short message can be interpreted differently without tone of voice or facial expressions.

    Example: “A brief reply such as ‘Fine’ might sound annoyed even when the sender meant it kindly.”

  6. Distraction During Face-to-Face Conversations

    What to write: Describe how notifications or frequent phone checking may affect attention when people are together.

    Example: “A friend may feel ignored if someone checks their phone throughout lunch.”

  7. Online Conflict and Respect

    What to write: Explore why people may speak differently online and how respectful communication can prevent harm.

    Example: “Before posting an angry reply, a student could pause and ask whether a private conversation would be more helpful.”

  8. Privacy and Oversharing

    What to write: Discuss the importance of choosing what to share and with whom, especially in group chats or public posts.

    Example: “Sharing a friend's personal news in a group chat without asking could damage trust.”

  9. Finding a Healthy Balance

    What to write: Suggest when digital communication works well and when a call or in-person talk may be better.

    Example: “A quick text is useful for making plans, while a sensitive disagreement may need a fuller conversation.”

  10. Conclusion: Does Technology Help or Hurt?

    What to write: Give a clear judgment based on the points you discussed. Explain the conditions under which technology improves communication.

    Example: “Technology can improve communication when people use it thoughtfully, give others their attention, and choose the right way to talk.”

Writing tip: Turn each subtitle into a paragraph. Make a clear point, add an example, and explain how it supports your argument. Include at least one strong point for the other side and respond to it fairly. Combine related sections if your teacher asks for a shorter essay.
