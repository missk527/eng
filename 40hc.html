Why Homelessness Needs More Attention

Use these ten subtitles to plan your essay. For each section, answer the guiding question in your own words and add a specific example. The example sentences are starting points; adapt them to your own argument. Write about people experiencing homelessness with dignity and avoid stereotypes.

  1. Introduction: Why This Issue Matters

    What to write: Introduce homelessness and state why your community should pay attention to it.

    Example: “Having a safe place to live affects many parts of daily life, yet people without stable housing are often overlooked.”

  2. What Homelessness Can Look Like

    What to write: Explain that homelessness can include staying in shelters, temporary places, or with others when there is nowhere permanent to live.

    Example: “A family staying temporarily with relatives may still be struggling to find a stable home.”

  3. Why People May Lose Housing

    What to write: Describe several possible causes, such as rising housing costs, loss of income, or family difficulties. Avoid blaming people for complex circumstances.

    Example: “If rent rises while a person's income falls, keeping a home may become difficult.”

  4. Challenges in Everyday Life

    What to write: Consider how a lack of stable housing can make it harder to store belongings, travel, prepare food, or plan ahead.

    Example: “Without a secure place for important documents, completing everyday tasks may become more complicated.”

  5. Effects on Children and Education

    What to write: Explain how moving frequently or lacking a quiet place can affect a student's school routine.

    Example: “A child who changes temporary accommodation often may have difficulty keeping the same journey to school.”

  6. Health, Safety, and Support

    What to write: Discuss why access to safe shelter and appropriate health and support services matters. Use a reliable source for specific health claims.

    Example: “A safe place to sleep and a clear way to reach support services can make daily life more manageable.”

  7. Why Stereotypes Are Harmful

    What to write: Challenge the idea that everyone experiencing homelessness has the same story. Explain why respectful language matters.

    Example: “It is unfair to assume why a person has lost housing without knowing their circumstances.”

  8. Immediate Help and Long-Term Solutions

    What to write: Compare urgent needs, such as shelter, with longer-term steps, such as stable housing and support finding work or services.

    Example: “An emergency shelter can provide a place for tonight, while help finding affordable housing can offer greater stability.”

  9. What Communities Can Do

    What to write: Suggest realistic actions for schools, local groups, and decision-makers. Explain who would do what.

    Example: “A school could organize a donation drive with a local shelter after asking which supplies are actually needed.”

  10. Conclusion: Making Housing a Priority

    What to write: Restate your main argument and summarize the most useful ways to respond.

    Example: “Homelessness deserves attention because stable housing supports safety and opportunity. Communities can respond with respect, immediate help, and lasting solutions.”

Writing tip: Turn each subtitle into a paragraph. State one point, give a concrete example, and explain how it supports your argument. If you include statistics or describe local services or policies, check a reliable source and cite it. Combine related sections if your teacher asks for a shorter essay.
