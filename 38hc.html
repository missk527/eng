The Impact of Fast Fashion on the Environment

Use these ten subtitles to plan your essay. Answer each guiding question in your own words, then add a specific example. Adapt the example sentences to fit your argument.

  1. Introduction: What Is Fast Fashion?

    What to write: Explain the term and introduce your main argument about its environmental effects.

    Example: “Fast fashion offers new styles frequently at low prices, but making and discarding so many clothes can put pressure on the environment.”

  2. Why Clothes Are Bought and Replaced So Quickly

    What to write: Discuss trends, low prices, and advertising as possible reasons people buy more than they need.

    Example: “A shopper might buy a new outfit for a short-lived trend even though they already own similar clothes.”

  3. The Resources Needed to Make Clothing

    What to write: Consider the water, land, energy, and raw materials involved in making fabric and garments.

    Example: “A cotton T-shirt begins with a crop that needs land and water before the fabric is even made.”

  4. Water Use and Water Pollution

    What to write: Describe how fabric production, dyeing, and finishing may affect water. Support specific claims with a reliable source.

    Example: “Dyeing fabric can require water, so factories need effective ways to treat wastewater.”

  5. Energy Use and Transport

    What to write: Trace the stages of a garment's journey and explain where energy may be used.

    Example: “A jacket may be made from materials produced in one place, sewn in another, and shipped to shops elsewhere.”

  6. Textile Waste and Landfills

    What to write: Explain what can happen when clothes are worn only a few times and then thrown away.

    Example: “A shirt bought for one event may end up in the trash while it is still wearable.”

  7. Synthetic Fabrics and Tiny Plastic Fibers

    What to write: Explain that some synthetic fabrics can shed small fibers during washing and discuss why this raises environmental concerns.

    Example: “Washing a polyester garment can release tiny fibers that may travel through wastewater.”

  8. Making Clothes Last Longer

    What to write: Suggest practical ways to use clothing for longer, such as caring for it, repairing it, or swapping it.

    Example: “Sewing on a missing button can make a favorite coat wearable again.”

  9. What Shoppers and Brands Can Do

    What to write: Give realistic actions for both consumers and clothing companies. Explain who is responsible for each change.

    Example: “Shoppers can choose items they expect to wear often, while brands can design durable clothes and share information about production.”

  10. Conclusion: Choosing a More Sustainable Approach

    What to write: Restate your main argument and summarize the environmental problems and solutions you find most important.

    Example: “Buying fewer clothes, wearing them longer, and improving how they are made can help reduce the environmental pressure of fast fashion.”

Writing tip: Turn each subtitle into a paragraph. State a point, give a concrete example, and explain how it supports your argument. If you include numbers or claims about a particular material or company, check a reliable source and cite it. Combine related sections if your teacher asks for a shorter essay.
