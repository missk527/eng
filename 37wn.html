The Dangers of Social Media Addiction

Use these ten subtitles to plan your essay. For each section, answer the guiding question in your own words and add a specific example. The example sentences are starting points; adapt them to your own argument.

  1. Introduction: When Use Becomes Excessive

    What to write: Introduce social media and explain what you mean by excessive use. State your main argument without assuming that every user has a problem.

    Example: “Social media helps people stay connected, but using it so often that it disrupts daily life can create problems.”

  2. Why Social Media Can Be Hard to Put Down

    What to write: Describe features such as endless feeds, notifications, or frequent updates that may encourage repeated checking.

    Example: “A student may open an app for one message and keep scrolling through new posts much longer than planned.”

  3. Lost Time and Distraction

    What to write: Explain how frequent checking can interrupt homework, hobbies, or responsibilities.

    Example: “Checking notifications every few minutes can make it harder to finish a reading assignment.”

  4. Effects on Sleep

    What to write: Discuss how late-night scrolling may reduce time available for sleep and affect the next day.

    Example: “Someone who plans to sleep at ten might keep watching videos until midnight and feel tired at school.”

  5. Comparing Ourselves with Others

    What to write: Explain how carefully chosen posts may lead people to compare their everyday lives with someone else's highlights.

    Example: “A student may feel left out after seeing photos from an event without knowing the full story behind them.”

  6. Pressure for Likes and Approval

    What to write: Describe how focusing too much on reactions to posts can shape a person's choices or feelings.

    Example: “Someone might delete a photo they liked simply because it received fewer likes than expected.”

  7. Online Conflict and Cyberbullying

    What to write: Discuss how constant access to online conversations can make hurtful messages difficult to escape. Avoid sharing anyone's private story without permission.

    Example: “A mean comment posted after school can continue to affect a student when they return home.”

  8. Less Time for Offline Connections

    What to write: Show how excessive screen time can crowd out in-person conversation, exercise, or other enjoyable activities.

    Example: “Friends at lunch may miss a chance to talk when everyone spends the break looking at their phones.”

  9. Building Healthier Habits

    What to write: Suggest practical steps, such as turning off some notifications, setting device-free times, or asking a trusted adult for support if use feels hard to control.

    Example: “Putting a phone away during homework can help a student focus and check messages afterward.”

  10. Conclusion: Using Social Media with Balance

    What to write: Restate your argument, summarize the most important risks, and end with a realistic recommendation.

    Example: “Social media can be useful, but setting boundaries helps protect time for sleep, learning, and relationships.”

Writing tip: Turn each subtitle into a paragraph. State a clear point, give a concrete example, and explain how it supports your argument. If you include statistics or health claims, check a reliable source and cite it. Combine related sections if your teacher asks for a shorter essay.
