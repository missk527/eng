The Need for Better Mental Health Support in Schools

Use these ten subtitles to plan your essay. For each section, answer the guiding question in your own words and add a specific example. These example sentences are starting points; adapt them to your school and your argument.

  1. Introduction: Why Student Well-Being Matters

    What to write: Introduce the topic and state why schools should support students' mental health.

    Example: “School is a place for learning, but students also need to feel safe and supported when life becomes difficult.”

  2. Pressures Students Face

    What to write: Identify a few pressures students may experience, such as exams, friendships, or family changes. Avoid assuming everyone has the same experience.

    Example: “A student may feel overwhelmed while preparing for exams and trying to manage activities outside school.”

  3. How Well-Being Affects Learning

    What to write: Explain how stress or worry can make schoolwork and participation harder.

    Example: “When a student is worried about a problem at home, it may be harder to concentrate in class.”

  4. Making It Easier to Ask for Help

    What to write: Suggest ways schools can make asking for support feel comfortable and straightforward.

    Example: “A school could explain clearly where students can go when they want to talk privately with a trusted adult.”

  5. Access to Trained Counselors

    What to write: Describe why students should know how to reach a qualified counselor or appropriate support service.

    Example: “If a student needs extra help, a school counselor can listen and help the student find suitable support.”

  6. Teaching Mental Health Awareness

    What to write: Explain how age-appropriate lessons can teach students to recognize emotions, respect others, and seek help.

    Example: “A class lesson could discuss healthy ways to handle everyday stress and how to support a friend without trying to solve everything alone.”

  7. Creating a Supportive School Culture

    What to write: Discuss respectful behavior, anti-bullying efforts, and the importance of belonging.

    Example: “Students may feel more comfortable at school when teachers respond to bullying and classmates include one another.”

  8. Working with Families and Communities

    What to write: Show how schools can communicate with families and connect students to community resources while respecting privacy.

    Example: “A school could share a list of local support services with families so they know where to turn when needed.”

  9. Challenges and Practical Solutions

    What to write: Acknowledge a challenge, such as limited time or funding, and suggest a realistic first step.

    Example: “If a school cannot add a counselor immediately, it could first make existing services easier to find and train staff to direct students to them.”

  10. Conclusion: A School Where Students Can Thrive

    What to write: Restate your position and summarize the most useful changes you proposed.

    Example: “Clear paths to support, caring adults, and a respectful school culture can help students feel ready to learn.”

Writing tip: Turn each subtitle into a paragraph. State a point, give a concrete example, and explain why it supports your argument. If you include statistics, check a reliable source and cite it. You can combine related sections for a shorter essay.
