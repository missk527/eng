Why Teenagers Should Learn Financial Literacy

Use these ten subtitles to plan your essay. Answer each guiding question in your own words and add a specific example. The example sentences are starting points; adapt them to your own argument.

  1. Introduction: What Is Financial Literacy?

    What to write: Define financial literacy as understanding everyday money choices and explain why teenagers can benefit from learning it.

    Example: “Learning how to plan spending and saving can help teenagers make thoughtful choices as they gain independence.”

  2. Understanding Income and Expenses

    What to write: Explain the difference between money coming in and money going out.

    Example: “A student who earns money from a weekend job can list what they receive and what they spend on transport and snacks.”

  3. Making a Simple Budget

    What to write: Show how a basic plan helps someone decide what they can afford and what to set aside.

    Example: “If a student has a limited amount for the month, a budget can help them plan for school supplies before entertainment.”

  4. Saving for Goals

    What to write: Explain how choosing a goal can make saving more purposeful.

    Example: “Setting aside a small part of each allowance or paycheck could help a teenager pay for a bicycle.”

  5. Needs, Wants, and Advertising

    What to write: Discuss how to tell an essential purchase from an optional one and how advertisements can influence decisions.

    Example: “Before buying a new pair of shoes because of an online ad, a student could ask whether the shoes they own still work.”

  6. Comparing Prices and Value

    What to write: Explain why the cheapest price is not always the best choice; consider quality, size, and how long an item will last.

    Example: “Comparing the price and durability of two school bags can help a student choose one that suits their needs.”

  7. Understanding Borrowing and Interest

    What to write: Introduce the idea that borrowing money usually means paying it back, sometimes with an additional cost. Avoid detailed product advice.

    Example: “A student should understand the full amount they would need to repay before agreeing to borrow money.”

  8. Staying Safe from Money Scams

    What to write: Explain why people should protect personal information and pause before responding to offers that seem too good to be true.

    Example: “If a message asks for bank details to claim a prize, a teenager can check with a trusted adult instead of clicking the link.”

  9. Preparing for More Independence

    What to write: Describe everyday situations in which money skills may help as teenagers grow older.

    Example: “Planning for transport, food, and study materials can help a student understand the cost of a new routine.”

  10. Conclusion: A Useful Life Skill

    What to write: Restate your position and summarize the skills you think schools or families should teach first.

    Example: “Financial literacy helps teenagers plan, compare choices, and ask informed questions about money.”

Writing tip: Turn each subtitle into a paragraph. State one point, give a concrete example, and explain how it supports your argument. If you mention specific financial products, interest rates, or laws, check an up-to-date reliable source for your location and cite it. Combine related sections for a shorter essay.
